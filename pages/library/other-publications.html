External & Military Publications

Civil Air Patrol Honor Guards execute ceremonial standards and protocols adapted directly from Department of the Air Force (DAF) doctrine, Department of Defense references, and joint-service manuals. The following external publications serve as primary authorities when CAP publications defer to military standards.

Department of the Air Force Doctrine

Doctrinal Distinction: Standard color guards utilize DAFPAM 34-1203 for quick-time regulation marching. Dedicated Civil Air Patrol Honor Guard units execute ceremonial drill derived from DAFMAN 34-165.
  • DAFMAN 34-165 – Honor Guards: Primary procedural manual for the USAF Honor Guard and Base Honor Guards. Defines ceremonial movements, slow cadence timing, high-lift mark time, casket watch protocol, and ceremonial uniform maintenance.
  • DAFPAM 34-1203 – Drill and Ceremonies: Baseline Air Force and Space Force regulation drill manual (formerly AFMAN 36-2203). Governs standard marching commands, formations, inspections, and regulation color guard maneuvers.
  • AFPAM 34-1202 – Guide to Protocol: Comprehensive guidance on official ceremonies, precedence of dignitaries, order of flags, receiving lines, and military funeral honors.
  • AFI 34-1201 – Protocol: Establishes official Air Force policies regarding ceremonial decorum, flag etiquette, and formal military functions.

POW/MIA Missing Man Table Packet

The National League of POW/MIA Families ceremony packet establishes the standardized layout, script, and table setup used across the Department of Defense and Civil Air Patrol. All ceremonial elements must preserve the solemnity and precise symbolism of this protocol:

Component Symbolic Meaning Ceremonial Placement
Round Table Represents our everlasting concern for our missing comrades Centered in front of the audience or head dais
White Tablecloth Symbolizes the purity of their motives in answering the call to arms Draped cleanly and evenly across the table
Single Red Rose Reminds us of the lives of these Americans and the loved ones who keep the faith In a slender vase tied with a yellow ribbon
Yellow Ribbon Symbolizes continued uncertainty, hope for their return, and determination to account for them Tied around the vase holding the red rose
Slice of Lemon & Salt Lemon represents their bitter fate; salt symbolizes the tears shed by families awaiting answers Placed on the bread plate
Inverted Glass Symbolizes their inability to share a toast with us today Placed upside down on the right side of the setting
Lone Candle Reflects the frailty of a prisoner alone against oppressors and the hope for their return Lit in the center of the setting
Empty Chair Signifies they are missing and absent from our gathering Tilted slightly toward the table

For the complete ceremony script and reading instructions, reference the Ceremonial Scripts & Protocols page.

Joint Service Weapon Manuals

Because Air Force manuals historically deferred to Army doctrine for manual-of-arms sequences, the following joint publications govern armed details and non-firing replica rifles:

  • TC 3-21.5 – Drill and Ceremonies (U.S. Army): Primary doctrinal authority for weapon manual of arms, color guard rifle bearer movements, and fixed-position commands with parade rifles.
  • MCO P5060.20 – Marine Corps Drill and Ceremonies Manual: Supplementary reference for joint color guard details and advanced ceremonial rifle sequences.
  • Title 10, U.S.C. § 9443: Federal statute authorizing the Secretary of the Air Force and federal agencies to supply equipment, materials, and support to Civil Air Patrol.
