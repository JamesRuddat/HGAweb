Academy & Program History

The First National Honor Guard Academy (2002)

Account by Noah Lorang — Basic Student, Class of 2002

The National Honor Guard Academy (NHGA) completed its first summer as a National activity in 2002. Prior to becoming one, the Academy spent one year as a Maryland Wing activity and one year as a Middle East Region activity. The mission of the Academy is to produce the finest honor guardsmen in Civil Air Patrol.

The Academy is a two-week program that, for basic students, is filled with an intensive curriculum of drill and ceremonies, etiquette, flag history, and various other activities designed to create honor guardsmen. In 2002, the Academy was privileged to have members of the United States Air Force Honor Guard based at Bolling AFB and members of the Langley AFB Base Honor Guard present for the entire two weeks to instruct cadets of all levels in drill.

Most of the drill instruction was conducted by the Air Force honor guardsmen, with assistance provided to basic students by intermediate and advanced students. The military training instructor, Colonel Dave Friedenburg, was responsible for the perfection of each cadet's drill and led ceremony training. Academic classes were taught by various Academy senior staff members and tactical officers.

Command Structure & Culture

The structure of the Academy differed from many cadet activities in that there were no cadet staff members. While more advanced students occasionally assisted in instruction, the chain of command went directly from each cadet to their tactical officer.

Rather than a high-stress, confrontational environment typical of initial encampments, the Academy was centered entirely on learning. The foundational disciplines learned at encampments were assumed as a baseline, allowing a massive volume of advanced material to be covered over two weeks.

Daily Routine & Training Schedule

The operational schedule was regular and demanding:

  • 0500 / 0515: Reveille and Physical Training (alternating days)
  • 0645: Morning formation and march to chow
  • Morning: Flight self-practice followed by USAF Honor Guard instruction
  • Afternoon: Classroom academics and advanced drill with USAF cadre
  • Evening: Drill flight time, dinner, followed by tactical officer "directed time"
  • 2200: Taps / Lights out

Three times during the two weeks, the entire academy conducted morale trips to the beach to decompress from training, including a barbecue following barracks inspections. Mid-way through the session, the Delaware Attorney General visited the activity to present basic cadets with their authentic parade rifles, retiring the wooden practice rifles they had trained on.

The Power of Teamwork: An honor guardsman cannot dress themselves—it requires two to three teammates working together to align and secure uniform distinctives. As noted in the Bearer's Creed: "Every action is sharp, crisp, and well-rehearsed; for we are a team, and together we are one."

Author Note: Noah Lorang joined the Chapel Hill Composite Squadron (North Carolina Wing) and served two years on the color guard before attending the 2002 National Honor Guard Academy as a basic student.

Regional Cadet Honor Academies & The 2013 Strategic Pause

Interim Guidance — Curt LaFond (NHQ/CP) & Col Craig Treadwell (CAP/CP), December 2012

The National Honor Guard Academy successfully introduced hundreds of cadets to honor guard principles over many years thanks to a dedicated cadre of volunteers. However, National Commander Maj Gen Carr announced a strategic pause for the centralized NHGA model in 2013 due to financial and demographic factors:

Operational Factor National Model (NHGA) Regional Model (CHA / HGA)
Tuition & Cost ~$500 tuition (twice standard national activities) Significantly lower registration & local travel
NHQ Subsidies $12,500 annually ($125/cadet) Self-sustaining within regional structures
Geographic Reach 75% Eastern US; only 20 Western cadets (2012) Accessible within driving range across all 8 regions
Slot Utilization Over 30% first-year student slots unfilled in 2012 Scalable capacity based on local wing demand

The Regional Model

Beginning in Summer 2013, Civil Air Patrol transitioned from a single centralized Maryland encampment to regional cadet honor academies hosted by individual regions. This regional distribution allows more cadets to participate without prohibitive airfare and tuition costs, while providing HGA alumni with local leadership opportunities to instruct, direct, and establish honor guards within their home wings.
